# E3 - Older people: pensions, BPC, poverty and health costs (interlinks)

Owner: Codex (microsimulation extension, analysis, report), Claude (data, literature, review, Monte Carlo wiring).
Requested by the user 2026-10-21: "if reduction in spending ... will also impact the pension of older population, older
population with less income with raising cost for health care ... should induce more poverty ... investigate well those
interlinks". Applies identically to every scenario and programme (cuts and increases alike).

## Why
PNAD 2025 (lead-developer check): poverty below R$ 759 per capita is 40.6% for ages 0-14, 6.0% for 65+; without pension
and BPC income 65+ poverty would be 63.5%. 80.2% of people 65+ receive a pension, 7.2% BPC; 59.2% of 65+ pensions are at
~1 minimum wage. The reported mid/high cut scenarios (Folha 2026-10-08; denied by the campaign) index INSS and BPC to INPC
only (no real minimum-wage gain); the Lula scenarios keep the real-gain rule. The current microsimulation (`src/micro.py`)
has no age dimension, applies `pension_and_indexation` to pension income only (BPC sits with Bolsa Família in
`means_tested_transfers`), and the model has no older-people health-cost channel.

## Protocol (fixed)
Data: `data/processed/brazil/microdata/pnadc_2025_c3_input.csv` (git-ignored, present locally; read only), IBGE price
indices `data/raw/brazil/rates/ibge_sidra_{1419,7060,1100,7063}_*.json` (IPCA/INPC general, health group, medicines,
medical services, health plans; monthly variation, 2012-2026), C1 `data/brazil/age_profiles.csv` (health relative cost
by age), `data/brazil/population.csv` (IBGE projection), `config/programs_2026.yaml` (scenario definitions).

1. Baseline profile (2025): poverty at R$ 218 and R$ 759 by age group (0-14, 15-29, 30-64, 65+); income composition
   by age group and income decile; share of household income from pensions + BPC; people of any age living with a
   pension/BPC recipient aged 65+ (and their poverty with and without that income). Excluded household conditions
   17-19 as in C3.
2. Microsimulation extension (`src/micro.py`, `tests/test_micro.py`; keep every existing output and default identical
   unless stated):
   (a) carry age through `PreparedMicrodata`; `calculate_indicators` adds poverty (both lines) for ages 0-14 and 65+,
       and for people living with a 65+ pension/BPC recipient;
   (b) minimum-wage-linked benefits: new option `bpc_follows_pension_indexation` (default True). When True, the
       `pension_and_indexation` gap is allocated over pension income AND BPC income in proportion to their weighted
       totals (current law links both to the minimum wage, so an indexation change moves both); under
       `indexation_floor`, BPC is entirely minimum-wage-linked. Symmetric: a negative gap (spending above S0, e.g.
       real minimum-wage gains) raises both. Report the C3 outputs for a stylised check with the option on and off.
3. Indexation arithmetic (deterministic, documented): real value of a minimum-wage benefit 2026-2035 under (i) current
   law (real gain 0.6-2.5%/yr: report 0.6, central 1.5, 2.5), (ii) INPC only. Cumulative gap by 2030 and 2035.
4. Health prices: annualised 2012-2026 inflation of IPCA and INPC general vs health group, medicines, medical services,
   health plans. "Health-adjusted" real change of an INPC-indexed benefit for (a) a household with INPC weights and
   (b) an older household with health weight doubled (sensitivity) and (c) plan holders vs non-holders: derive the
   implied annual erosion. State that medicines rose less than INPC (regulated prices) and plans more.
5. SUS per older person: age-weighted population (C1 health relative-cost profile x IBGE population) 2025-2045; real
   public health spending per age-weighted person under (i) S0 health path (from C1 baseline, `data/brazil/baseline.csv`
   category health), (ii) health spending held constant in real terms from 2027 (floor indexed to inflation only, the
   reported mid/high measure), (iii) health spending rising with revenue (current-law floor, revenue/GDP constant ->
   grows with GDP: use S0 GDP growth from `data/brazil/macro.csv` if present, else 1%/yr, flagged).
6. Literature (Claude provides `docs/elderly_review.md`; Codex cites it, does not write it).
