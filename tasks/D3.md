# D3 - Cross-country debt trajectories (50 countries, 1975-2024) vs the S0 debt path

Owner: Codex (panel build, analysis, report), Claude (data download, literature review, review, any model change).
Requested by the user 2026-10-21: "the debt is growing in S0 always ... review the 50 most relevant countries'
last 50 years of debt trajectories to see if you find something worth noting".

## Why
S0 general-government gross debt goes from 78.6% of GDP (2025) to a median 98.6% (2030), 130% (2035) and
244% (2045); 314% in 2045 with the C5 risk-premium feedback. Its primary balance falls to -2.4% (2030),
-4.0% (2035) and -7.7% (2045) because ageing raises spending (40 -> 46.7% GDP) while revenue is held and
no fiscal reaction is modelled. For comparison, IFI RAF 113 (June 2026) projects 111.6% in 2035 in its base
case (optimistic 80.9%, pessimistic 148.1%), with a primary deficit near 1% of GDP. D3 asks whether any
country's history looks like S0, and what the historical drivers of debt paths were.

- Cross-checks only: WEO `imf_GGXWDG_NGDP.json` (use years <= 2024), HPDD `imf_DEBT1.json`, GDD `imf_GG_DEBT_GDP.json`.

**Analyses.**
1. Debt-dynamics decomposition, every country-year with d_t, d_{t-1}, pb, ie, real growth g and deflator pi:
   gamma = (1+g)(1+pi)-1 (nominal growth). Contributions (pp GDP): primary deficit = -pb;
   interest = ie (interest is already % of current GDP); real growth = -g/(1+gamma) * d_{t-1};
   inflation = -(gamma-g)/(1+gamma) * d_{t-1}; stock-flow adjustment SFA = delta d minus the sum.
   Effective nominal rate i = ie (1+gamma) / d_{t-1}; r - g = (1+i)/(1+gamma) - 1 expressed as
   (i - gamma)/(1 + gamma). Drop i when d_{t-1} < 5% (unstable). Report descriptive statistics and flag
   |SFA| > 10 pp (defaults, bank bailouts, revaluations), listing them.
2. Trajectories. For every country, all 20-year windows: change in debt, longest run of consecutive annual
   increases, cumulative primary balance. Percentile of S0's 2025-2045 change (+165.8 pp, median path) and of
   S0's 2045 primary balance (-7.7) within the panel distributions, overall and for emerging only. List the
   ten largest 20-year increases with their decomposition. Peaks: local maxima of debt (highest in a
   +/-5-year window); distribution of debt 5 and 10 years after the peak.
3. Fiscal reaction function (Bohn 1998; Mendoza-Ostry 2008; Ghosh et al. 2013). Primary specification:
   pb_it = a_i + rho * d_{i,t-1} + beta * gap_it + phi * pb_{i,t-1} + e_it, gap = log real GDP deviation
   from an HP trend (lambda 100) built from WDI growth; country fixed effects; standard errors clustered by
   country. Report full panel, advanced, emerging, and Brazil alone (OLS with HAC SE, small-sample warning).
   Robustness: cubic in d_{t-1} (fiscal fatigue: the debt level where the marginal response turns down);
   without lagged pb; 1990-2024. Report the long-run response rho/(1-phi).
4. r - g. Distribution by group and decade; within-country persistence (AR(1)); slope of r-g on d_{t-1}
   with country FE. Brazil's average r-g by decade and its rank among the 50. JST robustness: r-g for the
   18 JST economies with the long-term rate (ltrate) and nominal GDP growth, 1950-2020.
5. Large debt-reduction episodes: from a peak, debt falls >= 15 pp within 10 years; large-increase
   episodes: debt rises >= 30 pp within 10 years. Decompose each episode into primary balance, real growth,
   inflation, interest, SFA. Summary by group.
6. Ageing and spending. Over 20-year windows (and 1995-2024 long difference), regress change in primary
   expenditure (pp GDP) and change in primary balance on change in the 65+ share, with group dummies. Compare
   the slope with S0's implied ratio (S0 change in spending_pct_gdp 2025-2045 divided by Brazil's 65+ share
   change 2025-2045). Report whether countries with fast ageing let the primary balance deteriorate as S0 does.
7. Brazil. Full decomposition 1975-2024 (data permitting), its fiscal reaction estimate, r-g against peers,
   and the S0 vs IFI RAF 113 comparison (numbers in "Why").
8. Implications. A short list of candidate S0/model changes, each tied to evidence above, for the lead
   developer to decide: e.g. an S0 variant with an estimated fiscal reaction, r-g calibration, SFA mean and
   variance, risk-premium feedback ceiling, inflation channel. Do not change any model file.
