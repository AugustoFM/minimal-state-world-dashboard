# T1 - Tax incidence by instrument in the microsimulation (symmetric)

Owner: Codex (implementation, tests, report), Claude (review, runs). Requested by the lead after the programme review
(2026-10-21): the C3 microsimulation spreads every revenue change over households with one rule
(`tax_variant`: proportional or consumption-like), so a tax rise on top incomes is charged partly to poor households
and a VAT cut is credited partly to the rich in the same proportion. Incidence must depend on the **instrument**, not on
the programme, and the same instrument must have the same incidence whether the tax rises or falls.

## Instruments (incidence bases; all from data already in the repo)
| instrument | base in PNAD 2025 (`data/processed/brazil/microdata/pnadc_2025_c3_input.csv`) | note |
|---|---|---|
| `top_income` | income above a threshold: households whose per-capita income puts members in the top 1% of persons; burden proportional to income above the top-1% cut | PNAD under-reports top and capital incomes; report the IRPF 2024 top-1% share (`data/brazil/micro/tax_centiles_2024.csv`, existing `tax_data_context`) next to the PNAD share and state that the PNAD base understates the top. A tax on top incomes is allocated entirely within the top 1% (capacity-capped like the existing `_tax_burden`) |
| `consumption` | existing consumption-like score (`consumption_like_exponent`) | VAT-type taxes |
| `fuel_energy` | consumption-like score (same exponent) | no POF energy budget shares in the repo: flagged gap, same as consumption |
| `payroll` | labour income of employed persons (`labor_income_nominal_brl`, `employment_status` = employed) | formal/informal split not available in the prepared file: flagged; burden on wages |
| `tax_expenditures` | proportional to household income (existing `proportional`) | mixed beneficiaries; no measure-level data |
| `business_capital` | allocated like `top_income` | exports/investment exemptions benefit capital owners; contested, flagged |

## Programme measure shares (assumptions, documented in `config/programs_2026.yaml`; one place)
- L5 (revenue up, 0 to +0.5 pp GDP): `top_income` 0.5, `tax_expenditures` 0.5 (programme: "more tax on top incomes, fewer
  tax expenditures"; the IR exemption to R$ 5,000 is current law and already in S0).
- F3 (revenue down, 0 to -1.0 pp GDP): `consumption` 0.4, `fuel_energy` 0.2, `payroll` 0.3, `business_capital` 0.1
  (programme: lower VAT rate, energy and fuel taxes, payroll cost, exports and investment).
- Sensitivity `--tax-incidence uniform`: the previous behaviour (every instrument follows the draw's `tax_variant`), so
  the effect of the change can be reported.

## Implementation
1. `src/micro.py`: `simulate_household_incomes` accepts `revenue_change_by_instrument_pp: Mapping[str, float] | None`
   (default None = current behaviour, bit-identical). Each instrument's change is allocated with its base; a revenue
   rise is a burden (capacity-capped as now), a fall is a credit with the same base. Unknown instrument -> ValueError.
2. `src/programs.py`: programme scenarios carry `revenue_by_instrument_by_year` (pp GDP per instrument), built from the
   existing L5/F3 draws times the shares above; `revenue_by_year` remains their sum (macro unchanged).
3. `src/montecarlo.py` `micro_for_draw`: pass the instrument split for scenarios that have it; S0 and S1-S5 keep the
   current single-rule path. CLI `--tax-incidence {instrument,uniform}` (default `instrument`), recorded in metadata.
4. Report `outputs/tax_incidence.md` (neutral; instruments, not candidates; no programme results): for a stylised
   +/-0.1 pp GDP change per instrument, the change in poverty (R$ 218, R$ 759), Gini, top-1% and top-10% shares, and the
   share of the burden/credit by income decile; the PNAD vs IRPF top-1% comparison. `docs/tax_incidence.md`: method,
   assumptions, limitations (no behavioural response, PNAD under-reporting at the top, no POF consumption shares, payroll
   incidence fully on workers).

## Tests (`tests/test_micro.py`, `tests/test_programs.py`, `tests/test_montecarlo.py` as needed)
None = bit-identical; a `top_income` rise changes no household below the top-1% cut; symmetry: +x then -x on the same
instrument returns the original incomes (up to the capacity cap, which binds only for rises); allocations sum to the
target; `revenue_by_year` equals the sum of instruments; default Monte Carlo summary for S0 and S1-S5 bit-identical
(3 draws, written under the system temp).

## Outputs (Codex-owned; nothing else may be modified)
`src/micro.py`, `src/programs.py`, `src/montecarlo.py`, `config/programs_2026.yaml` (shares only),
`tests/test_micro.py`, `tests/test_programs.py`, `tests/test_montecarlo.py`, `outputs/tax_incidence.md`,
`docs/tax_incidence.md`. No network, no git.

## T1b - poverty measure at the line (lead review)

Lead check on PNAD 2025, excluding household conditions 17-19: 1.60% of persons and 2.86% of
people aged 65+ have per-capita household income exactly R$759.00, half the 2025 minimum wage.
Because the diagnostic headcount uses `income < line`, even a very small loss moves that entire mass
below the line. A 0.1 pp GDP consumption-tax diagnostic (about R$5 per person per month) therefore
raises the raw R$759 headcount by about 1.7 pp. This is a static-survey step artefact, not a welfare
effect of that size. There is no corresponding mass point at R$218.

Implementation and review requirements:

1. `calculate_indicators` retains each raw headcount and adds, for both poverty lines and all existing
   populations (all, age 0-14, age 65+, and living with a 65+ pension/BPC recipient), a headcount
   averaged over 21 equally spaced lines from 0.95 to 1.05 times the line, FGT1 as a percentage of the
   line, and FGT2.
2. `src/montecarlo.py` reports changes from S0 in the smoothed headcount and FGT1 for both lines and
   every population, plus FGT2 for all persons. `scripts/report_brazil_results.py` adds the smoothed
   R$759 and poverty-gap measures after the raw measures without removing existing output.
3. `docs/tax_incidence.md` explains the mass point and treats the smoothed headcount and FGT1 as the
   R$759 headline measures. `outputs/tax_incidence.md` reports raw, smoothed and FGT1 poverty changes
   in the stylised instrument table.
4. Synthetic tests cover the discontinuous raw headcount at a mass point, the smaller smoothed
   response, continuous FGT1, an analytic uniform-income case, symmetric allocations and zero S0
   changes. The default run may add indicators, but every existing row must remain identical.

Additional owned files for T1b: `scripts/report_brazil_results.py` and
`tests/test_report_brazil_results.py`. No network, no git.
