Four Years to Decide

Play out a hypothetical four-year presidency under either candidate’s own filed government programme, one year at a time. Every number comes from a real, published, open-source model of the Brazilian economy — nothing here is invented for the game.

Before you vote in the case: play this through once as Lula, once as Flávio Bolsonaro, with whatever year-by-year choices feel closest to how you’d expect each of them to actually govern. Then compare.

This single number adds up three things that are normally measured in different units — the debt path (already % of GDP), the jobs effect (converted via Brazil’s own Okun coefficient), and the lives effect (converted via the slider below) — onto one common GDP-equivalent scale, so they can be compared directly. This is a “value of a statistical life” (VSL) — a standard cost-benefit-analysis technique for weighing a population-level change in death risk against a fiscal number. It is not pricing any individual child.
R$5.5M per infant death averted — default from Brazil’s own published value-of-life research (IPEA/Stivali 2023, central estimate; range R$2.3M–R$12M). Move the slider and the breakdown below updates live.

Poverty and the Gini coefficient are shown below but not folded into this score — there is no agreed, sourced way to monetise them, and pretending otherwise would smuggle in an invented weight exactly where the other two terms don’t need one.

Companion tool to the teaching case “The Vote: Brazil’s 2026 Runoff.” Built on the same published Monte Carlo fiscal model as the case’s own Exhibits, reusing Brazil-specific sourced parameters (demography, fiscal multipliers, the Okun coefficient, household microsimulation). This is not a forecast of what either candidate will do — it plays out each candidate’s own filed programme under choices you make, not a prediction of actual governance.
